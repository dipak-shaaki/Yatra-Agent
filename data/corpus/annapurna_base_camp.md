# Annapurna Base Camp (ABC)

One of Nepal's most popular treks, leading into the dramatic Annapurna Sanctuary — a high-altitude amphitheater surrounded by Annapurna I, Machhapuchhre (Fishtail), and Hiunchuli. Well-established teahouses, moderate difficulty, and rich Gurung culture along the route make it a favorite introductory Himalayan trek.

## Best Time to Visit

Autumn (September–December) offers clear skies and the best mountain visibility. Spring (March–May) brings blooming rhododendron forests (Lali Guras, Nepal's national flower) and pleasant temperatures. Avoid monsoon (June–August), when trails become muddy and slippery and views are often obscured. Winter is cold near base camp but offers clear skies and quiet trails.

## Route and Access

The trailhead is at Nayapul, reached via Pokhara:
- **Kathmandu to Pokhara**: flight (~25 minutes) or drive (6–7 hours)
- **Pokhara to Nayapul**: short drive, then the trek begins

## Difficulty

Rated moderate. No technical climbing skills required, but the route includes long stretches of stone staircases (notably around Ulleri and Chhomrong) that are demanding on the knees. Expect 5–7 hours of walking per day. Altitude sickness risk exists at the 4,130m base camp but is generally lower than on higher treks like Everest Base Camp. Suitable for fit beginners with some pre-trip training.

## Duration

Typically 7–12 days; extending to 14 days if combined with the Ghorepani/Poon Hill route. Round-trip distance is roughly 110–120 km.

## Permits

- **Annapurna Conservation Area Permit (ACAP)** — NPR 3,000 for foreign nationals; Nepali citizens generally pay a much lower or no fee, confirm current rate at the ACAP office in Pokhara or Kathmandu
- **TIMS card** (Trekkers' Information Management System)

> **Note:** Sources disagree on current TIMS enforcement on Annapurna trails as of 2026 — some report it was abolished or is not being checked, others list it as an active requirement. Confirm before travel.

## Estimated Budget

Roughly **NPR 12,000–25,000 per person** for a 7-9 day trek, covering teahouse lodging, meals, local transport to/from Pokhara, and permits. Costs are lower for domestic travelers going without a guide (not currently mandatory for Nepali citizens on this route, though this should be reconfirmed) and higher if hiring a guide or porter. This is a rough estimate, not an official figure.

## Culture Notes

The route passes through traditional Gurung villages such as Ghandruk, known for stone houses and a cultural museum.
