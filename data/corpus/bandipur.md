# Bandipur

A well-preserved Newari hilltop settlement between Kathmandu and Pokhara, sitting on a ridge above the Marsyangdi River valley. Known for intricately carved traditional architecture, cobblestone streets, and Himalayan views on clear days, Bandipur is protected as a living heritage site and makes for a convenient, increasingly popular stop for travelers moving between Kathmandu and Pokhara.

## Best Time to Visit

Spring (March–May) brings warm weather, rhododendron blooms, and lush green hiking trails. Autumn (September–November) offers crisp air, sharp Himalayan views, golden rice-harvest terraces, and coincides with the Dashain and Tihar festivals. Avoid monsoon (June–August), which brings heavy rain, humidity, and obscured views.

## Route and Access

- Bus from Kathmandu to Dumre (5–6 hours) via the Prithvi Highway, then local bus/jeep 8km uphill to Bandipur
- Bus from Pokhara to Dumre (3–4 hours), then local transport to Bandipur
- Private car/taxi directly from Kathmandu or Pokhara

## Difficulty

Easy — the town itself requires no trekking. Nearby optional day-hikes include Ramkot village (6km, about 1.5 hours one-way, easy) and Siddha Gufa cave (recommended with a guide; some sections require climbing with rope assistance).

## Duration

1–2 days is generally recommended.

## Things to Do

- Explore Newari architecture and cobblestone streets
- Hike to Thani Mai temple for sunrise/sunset views
- Day hike to Ramkot village
- Visit Siddha Gufa cave, one of Nepal's largest caves
- Try Newari cuisine (samay baji, bara, choila) at local restaurants

## Estimated Budget

Roughly **NPR 3,000–6,000 per person per day**, covering bus fare from Kathmandu/Pokhara, guesthouse lodging, and meals. This is a rough domestic-traveler estimate for a 1-2 day trip, not an official figure.

## Practical Notes

There are no ATMs in Bandipur — bring cash from Kathmandu, Pokhara, or Dumre. The nearest medical facilities are in Dumre.

## Culture Notes

One of the few Newari towns outside Kathmandu Valley, founded in the 18th century by Newari merchants as a trade hub. Wealthy merchant homes and temples reflect this history.
