# Gorkha

Historic hilltop town and birthplace of King Prithvi Narayan Shah, who unified Nepal in the 18th century. The Gorkha Durbar (palace) complex combines royal palace, fort, and temple architecture atop a hill overlooking the Trisuli Valley, with panoramic views of Manaslu, Himalchuli, Ganesh Himal, and the Annapurna range. A UNESCO-recognized heritage site, rebuilt after damage from the 2015 earthquake.

## Best Time to Visit

October to May aligns with Nepal's main trekking season; spring (March–May) and autumn (September–November) are specifically recommended for clear skies and views. June to September is hot and humid, with monsoon clouds typically obscuring mountain views.

## Route and Access

- Regular buses and private vehicles from Kathmandu to Gorkha Bazaar (approximately 5–6 hours)
- Also reachable via Abu Khaireni on the Kathmandu–Pokhara highway route

From Gorkha Bazaar, climb approximately 1,500–1,700 stone steps (30–45 minutes) to reach Gorkha Durbar, or take a taxi/jeep via an alternate road to a shorter, less strenuous approach.

## Difficulty

Easy to moderate — the stair climb to the palace is steep but requires no trekking gear.

## Duration

A day trip is sufficient, or stay overnight for a more relaxed visit including sunrise views from the palace hilltop.

## Things to Do

- Explore the Gorkha Durbar palace complex and Gorkha Durbar Museum
- Visit the 17th-century Kalika Temple within the durbar grounds
- Watch sunrise over the Himalayas from the palace hilltop
- Explore Gorkha Bazaar market
- Try local dal bhat and other Nepali street food

## Practical Notes

The Gorkha Durbar site is generally open daily (commonly cited hours around 9am–5pm), with affordable entry fees for both domestic and international visitors. Hiring a local guide is optional but adds historical context.

## Estimated Budget

Roughly **NPR 3,000–6,000 per person per day**, covering bus fare from Kathmandu, basic lodging, meals, and the Durbar entry fee. This is a rough domestic-traveler estimate for a day trip or short overnight stay, not an official figure.

## Culture Notes

The site holds deep spiritual significance for Nepalis as the birthplace of unified Nepal; animal sacrifices to the goddess Kali still take place at the temple on significant lunar dates.
