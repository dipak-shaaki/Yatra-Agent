# Manaslu Circuit

A remote circuit trek around Mount Manaslu (8,163m), the world's eighth-highest mountain, through the Manaslu Conservation Area near the Tibetan border. Compared to Everest or Annapurna, Manaslu sees far fewer trekkers, and the trail passes through Gurung and Tibetan-influenced villages with Buddhist monasteries and mani walls, especially in the upper Nubri and Tsum areas. The trek's high point is the crossing of Larkya La Pass at 5,106m.

## Best Time to Visit

Autumn (September–November) offers the most stable weather and clearest mountain views. Spring (March–May) brings blooming rhododendron forests and milder temperatures. Avoid winter (December–February), when heavy snowfall can close Larkya La Pass, and monsoon (June–August), which brings rain, leeches, and landslides.

## Route and Access

The trailhead is at Machha Khola (some itineraries start at Soti Khola or Jagat). From Kathmandu, it's a 160–180 km road journey:
- **Public bus**: 9–12 hours from Kathmandu's Macha Pokhari bus park, via Dhading Besi and Arughat
- **Private jeep**: 7–10 hours, more flexible and comfortable

The circuit ends at Dharapani or Tiliche, with a jeep ride back to Kathmandu (8–10 hours via Besisahar).

## Difficulty

Rated moderate to difficult. Requires good physical fitness and proper acclimatization. Most trekking days involve 6–8 hours of walking on steep, rocky, sometimes exposed terrain. No technical climbing skills are needed. **Solo trekking is not permitted** — a minimum group size plus a licensed guide is required by law.

## Duration

12–18 days depending on itinerary; 14 days is the most common length, including acclimatization days. The full circuit trail is approximately 177 km.

## Permits

Three permits are required:
- **Manaslu Restricted Area Permit (RAP)** — required between Jagat and Dharapani
- **Manaslu Conservation Area Permit (MCAP)**
- **Annapurna Conservation Area Permit (ACAP)** — required for the final section near Dharapani

Permits must be arranged through a government-registered trekking agency; independent application is not allowed, and a licensed guide is mandatory.

> **Note:** As of March 2026, the historical two-trekker minimum group requirement was reportedly relaxed by Nepal's Department of Immigration, though a licensed guide remains mandatory. This rule area is still evolving — confirm current requirements with the Department of Immigration or a registered trekking agency before travel.

## Estimated Budget

Roughly **NPR 35,000–60,000 per person** for the full trek (14 days), covering guide, basic teahouse lodging, meals, permits, and transport to/from the trailhead. This is a rough domestic-traveler estimate, not an official figure — a licensed guide is legally mandatory here regardless of budget, and that cost alone is a significant share of the total. Foreign-tourist package prices quoted online (USD 700-1,100+) are not representative of domestic costs, since they typically bundle international-tourist-rate services.

## Culture Notes

The route passes through Gurung and Tibetan-influenced villages with active Buddhist monasteries and mani walls. Community-run teahouse lodging is available along most of the route.
